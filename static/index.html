Salary Terms in Plain English (Zero Jargon)

Everything you need to know about your Indian payslip, tax rules, and hidden employer clauses explained simply.

1. Basic Salary

The Foundation of Your Pay

Basic pay is the core fixed amount of your salary (usually 40% to 50% of your package). It is 100% taxable. Why it matters: Your Provident Fund (12%), Gratuity (4.81%), and HRA (40-50%) are calculated directly as percentages of this number. A lower basic means more in-hand cash today, but less retirement corpus.

2. House Rent Allowance (HRA)

Rent Subsidy Allowance

Provided by employers to cover accommodation costs. Standardly calculated as 50% of Basic Pay in metro cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata) and 40% in non-metros. Under the Old Tax Regime, HRA is partially or fully tax-exempt if you submit rent receipts. Under the New Tax Regime, HRA has no tax exemption.

3. Special / Flexi Allowance

The "Balancing Figure"

Special allowance is simply whatever money is left over after allocating Basic and HRA to reach your promised gross salary. It has no special tax exemptions and is 100% taxable at slab rates.

4. Provident Fund (EPF)

The 24% Retirement Deduction

Total statutory PF contribution is 24% of Basic Pay. 12% is paid by you (Employee PF), deducted from your monthly gross pay. 12% is paid by your employer (Employer PF), but almost every Indian company sneaks this into your headline CTC! If your basic is high, capping PF at the statutory ₹1,800/mo ceiling significantly boosts your monthly in-hand cash.

5. Gratuity (The 5-Year Catch)

You Don't Get It Unless You Stay 5 Years!

Under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, companies are required by law to pay 15 days of basic pay for every completed year of service. However, companies deduct ~4.81% of your basic from your headline CTC every month. If you leave the company before 5 continuous years, you forfeit this money completely!

6. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS)

Your Monthly Income Tax Installment

Instead of making you pay a huge tax bill at the end of March, your employer projects your annual tax under your chosen regime (New vs Old) and divides it by 12, withholding that exact amount from your monthly bank deposit.
